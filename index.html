01 — Project

The facility, the field and the contract

The Ad Daimah EPF is a rental facility: the Contractor designs, mobilises, installs, commissions, operates, maintains and finally removes it. The permanent CPF is not available at first oil, so the EPF must stand alone — power, gas, flare, water and export metering included.

Location and licence

The Ad Daimah contract area lies in south-west Iraq, Maysan Governorate, south of Amarah and roughly 85 km north-west of Basrah. It was awarded in May 2024 under Iraq’s fifth licensing round as a Development and Production Service Contract, with KAR OG Dima DMCC as operator. The block is defined by six boundary points in WGS 84 / UTM zone 38N (EPSG:32638) and covers approximately 448 km².

The exploration well Ad Daimah-1 (Da-1) was spudded in September 2011 and completed in August 2012. It encountered oil shows in the Harta, Sa’adi, Khasib, Mishrif, Nahr Umr and Yamama formations; drill-stem tests confirmed oil and gas in Sa’adi, Khasib, Mishrif and Yamama, while the Nahr Umr test failed at that time. Twenty-two production tests followed in 2017–2018, including successful Nahr Umr tests. That uncertainty — reservoir performance, fluid characterisation, asphaltene and sand behaviour — is exactly what the EPF and the extended well-test programme are there to resolve.

Why three trains, and why one location

Five surface-facility configurations were evaluated. The selected “distributed EPF” or reservoir-specific model gives each reservoir its own processing train, so that test data and fluid behaviour are never mixed. All three trains are nonetheless co-located at the M_NU10 hub wellpad, sharing one operations crew, one logistics base and one export route. Crude is stabilised per reservoir and only then comingled in the export flow tanks.

Yamama is kept separate because it is sour: the Scope of Work requires NACE/ISO-compliant metallurgy, a SIL 2-capable safety instrumented system and dedicated H₂S stripping. Raw Yamama gas must not enter common compression or fuel systems unless the whole system is rated for it.

Export route

Stabilised crude leaves M_NU10 through a permanent 10-inch trunkline to the CPF area, and from there through the permanent 16-inch export line to the Halfaya 42-inch pipeline valve station, where custody passes to the marketing agency. Two options are priced: Option A uses a blending tank plus booster pumps at M_NU10 and temporary CPF-site storage with dedicated export pumps; Option B places heavy-duty export pumps at M_NU10 and pumps the full route in one step. The selected option must be verified against Halfaya receiving pressure, pump shut-off head, surge and pipeline rating.

Margin & modularity

The +10% margin is applied once to the agreed design flows and reported separately from the nominal 25,000 bopd. Modules are sized for optimum economy, operability and relocation — and one train must be able to shut down without making the others unsafe.

Metallurgy

All process-wetted equipment is rated for the applicable sour service to NACE/ISO 15156, covering H₂S, CO₂, water chemistry and the approved pressure/temperature envelope. Qualification records are a deliverable, not an afterthought.

Availability

95% minimum rolling-average availability from handover. Sparing is N+1; the operating rate is reduced by 3% for every full or partial point below 95% in the previous calendar month.

03 — Process

From flowline to custody transfer

Each train is a complete oil facility: reception and sand removal, staged separation with heating, electrostatic dehydration and two-stage desalting, then stabilisation, storage, allocation metering and transfer to the common export system. Gas and water are treated inside the same battery limit.

Fig. 1 — Generic train, with Yamama-specific and optional scope marked. See area D for the sour-crude additions.

Mishrif / Khasib — 10,000 bopd

Two-stage separation with interstage heating, electrostatic dehydrator and two-stage desalting, flow tanks with off-spec reprocessing, allocation metering. Associated gas is mildly sour (~0.08% H₂S) and can feed common fuel gas if the whole system is rated for it.

  • Inlet heater 166 kW · dehyd heater 0.668 MW
  • Wash water ~1,255 bbl/d at 60 °C
  • Export API ~20 · salt 30 ppmw

Nahr Umr — 10,000 bopd

Same flowsheet, tuned for higher asphaltene and sand risk. Sand removal sits upstream of the heater-separator, and its HP separator off-gas is the preferred stripping gas source for the Yamama column — a genuine inter-train dependency that the design must be able to lose.

  • Inlet heater 137 kW · flow tanks 207 mbarg
  • PW 1,099 bbl/d · oil in water ~9.8 ppm
  • GOR lower: 3.06 MMSCFD separator gas

Yamama — 5,000 bopd, sour

Dedicated sour-service train: NACE/ISO 15156 materials, SIL 2-capable SIS, H₂S stripping to 10 ppmw before storage. Fuel gas, nitrogen for inerting, and a closed gas path to flare — raw Yamama gas never enters common fuel or compression.

  • Stripper: 500 mm, 2 m packing, 5 barg, 65 °C
  • Stripping gas: NU HP off-gas, ~0.66 MMSCFD
  • Produced water 483 bbl/d, salinity 155,000 mg/L
Two process clarifications we will raise in the bid
  1. Yamama desalting. The Scope of Work (§3.3.2) mandates 2-stage electrostatic desalting on the 5,000 bopd Yamama unit. The 2025 Execution Strategy notes that 5,000 bopd modules historically used filter-coalescer units instead. We price the Scope of Work configuration and flag the weight/plot impact.
  2. Stripping-gas dependency. Losing Nahr Umr production removes the preferred stripping gas. The design must include a local backup supply, and the operating envelope must define the production reduction or shutdown that follows if neither is available.

05 — Modularisation

How many skids does it take?

The answer has three layers: process skids (the equipment that does the work), electrical and ICSS packages (containers), and transport modules (what actually travels by road).

Table 5 — Skid and package count by area
AreaSkid / package MishrifNahr UmrYamama SharedTotal

Skids by area

Bars show total units per area. Shared packages are counted once and serve all three trains.

What counts as a skid

A structural frame with its equipment, piping, valves, instruments and local control, lifted and transported as one unit. Vessels too large for a skid (flow tanks, PW tanks) are field-erected and excluded from the count.

What travels as a module

Skids are grouped onto transport frames. At the Ad Daimah heavy-haul limits a 10,000 bopd train ships in roughly 9–11 modules and the Yamama train in 10–12, so the whole facility is about 38–46 road modules plus tank and flare erection crews.

Why the numbers move

Vendor packaging, the Company’s decision on sand handling (wellsite versus EPF inlet), the export option, and whether utilities are shared or per-train can each shift the total by ±10%. We hold a firm count after the equipment list is frozen.

Consequences we design to

  • Separators stay within about 12–14 m tangent length and 2.4–2.8 m diameter, so each stage is one module.
  • The H₂S stripper is a 500 mm column with 2 m of structured packing — it fits without splitting.
  • Export flow tanks are field-erected; skid-mounted tanks are used only for chemicals and slops.
  • Every frame is designed for repeated lifting and road transport, not just one installation.
  • De-watering is in the EPF scope wherever the civil design calls for deep foundations.

A heavy-haul route survey is a Contractor deliverable; its findings set the road rehabilitation scope Company must complete before mobilisation.

Train arrangement

Inlet and reception at the north edge, separation and treating running south, flow tanks and transfer at the south-east corner, flare at the plot boundary. Process flow moves north→south inside each pad so piping runs stay short and the export header collects at one end.

Shared versus dedicated

Utilities, power, control room, workshop and the export tank farm are shared — the Scope of Work permits shared utility packages where capacity and reliability are demonstrated. Separation, desalting, sour treatment, flare and metering stay dedicated to protect reservoir data.

Safety spacing

Flares, the flare KO drum and the sour train sit at the south boundary, downwind of the prevailing wind and remote from the control room and accommodation. Final spacing follows the QRA, fire and explosion analysis and HAC drawings.

07 — Power, control and safety

Electrical, ICSS and telecommunications

No CPF power, no CPF controls, no CPF gas. The EPF is an island grid with its own generation, protection, safety system and satellite link back to the well pads.

Generation and distribution

Generation
Independent, with full backup redundancy. Generators run synchronised, each loaded to ~50% so one set can carry the facility alone. Diesel or treated Mishrif/Nahr Umr gas.
Emergency
One prime-rated, containerised diesel genset per facility with back-up fuel storage and a remote HMI in the substation.
Voltages
MV 11 kV or 6.6 kV; LV 400 V three-phase / 230 V single-phase; motors ≥150 kW on MV. Power factor ≥0.95 lagging (0.98 with optional APFC).
Transformers
2 × 100% oil-immersed, 11(33)/0.424 kV, conservator, off-circuit tap changer, winding temperature and Buchholz monitoring.
Switchgear
Type-tested MV air-insulated withdrawable vacuum; LV metal-clad draw-out. IEC 61850 IEDs, RS-485 meters, all signals to the PMS/HMI.
UPS
Parallel redundant AC and DC UPS, batteries 2 × 50%, ≥2 h autonomy, separate air-conditioned battery room with two-door escape, gas monitoring, eyewash.
Enclosures
IP65 outdoors with a three-sided sun canopy; IP42 indoors, rated for continuous duty with HVAC failure. Stainless steel in hazardous areas.
Hazardous area
IECEx/ATEX certified; Ex-d/Ex-de motors; Zone 1, group IIC, T6 for battery rooms. Component-only “U” certificates are not accepted for standalone installation.
Studies
ETAP short-circuit, load flow, motor starting, protection coordination, earthing and lightning protection — native model files handed to Company.

ICSS, telecommunications and safety

Control
PLC/RTU-based DCS-SCADA with operator and engineering workstations, historian at 30-second intervals, PMS interface and package PLC integration.
Safety
SIS with SIL 2 capability, ESD logic, cause-and-effect, permissive and interlock testing. F&G detection and suppression interfaces per the fire and gas philosophy.
Field instruments
Emerson Rosemount type or equal, HART-capable: pressure, temperature, flow, level, analytical, vibration and package monitoring, with impulse lines and manifolds verified on site.
Integration
Wellhead control panels, RTUs and third-party packages over Modbus, OPC, Ethernet/IP or Profibus; fibre to each well pad with redundant paths and cyber-security settings.
Metering
Per-reservoir allocation metering plus the common custody-transfer interface. MPFM at each wellhead for well testing; lab sampling at agreed points.
Utilities
Instrument air (duty/standby compressors, dryers, receivers), nitrogen for inerting only — never for stripping, marshalling panels, earthing and lightning protection.
Testing
FAT, IFAT at the OEM works, SAT, calibration, loop checks, cause-and-effect verification, ESD and F&G tests, integrated system test — all witnessed.
Handover
As-installed drawings, certificates, calibration records, operating manuals and punch-list close-out archived in the CMMS.
Commissioning levels

Commissioning runs L1 to L5 with Company acceptance at every level: L1 mechanical completion and cleanliness, L2 subsystem checks and loop testing, L3 cold commissioning without hydrocarbons, L4 hydrocarbon introduction and stabilisation, L5 performance and reliability testing against Tables 4.1 and 4.1.1 of the Scope of Work.

08 — Execution

From award to first oil, then rental operation

A rented, pre-engineered facility compresses engineering but loads the front end: fitness-for-service of used equipment, refurbishment, heavy haul and a very short installation window.

  1. Mobilisation planning Month 0–1

    Execution plan, mobilisation schedule, heavy-haul route survey, organisation chart, CVs, site resourcing plan with nationalisation targets, insurance certificates, HSE plan.

  2. Engineering validation Month 0–4

    Update the integrated simulation and H&MB against the approved feed cases, issue PFDs, P&IDs, equipment list, datasheets, cause and effect, plot plan, load data and civil inputs. HAZID/HAZOP/LOPA and SIL verification with the actual M_NU10 fluids.

  3. Fitness for continued service Month 1–5

    Static inspection for corrosion, erosion and cracking; rotating equipment overhaul where idle over 12 months; valve function and leak-tightness; piping thickness survey; lifting gear certification; verification of critical spares and OEM support. Obsolete control systems need an approved mitigation plan.

  4. Refurbishment and packaging Month 2–8

    Overhaul, re-instrument, re-certify, paint, skid-build, integrate third-party packages and test at the works. FAT and IFAT witnessed by Company or its delegate.

  5. Mobilisation and installation Month 6–11

    Customs clearance, heavy haul from the Contractor base to M_NU10, setting on Company-provided foundation pads, piping and cable installation, mechanical completion. Civil works, roads and the evaporation pond are Company scope and must be ready.

  6. Commissioning and start-up Month 10–14

    L1–L5 commissioning, loop checks, cause-and-effect, ESD and F&G tests, inerting, hydrocarbon introduction after Company confirms power, stripping gas, flare, water outlets, export route and shutdown interfaces are ready.

  7. Performance test and handover First oil + 30–90 days

    Demonstrate 25,000 bopd, product specification, 20% turndown per train and 95% availability. Operator training and competency records handed over with the as-built documentation.

  8. Rental operation Until CPF transfer

    24/7 operations and maintenance, consumables, spares, chemicals, lab, catering, transport, accommodation, medical cover and ambulance, daily reporting, RCA on failures, KPI reporting.

  9. Decommissioning and demobilisation After changeover

    Hydrocarbon-freeing, isolation, dismantling, load-out and site restoration per the approved changeover plan, with the facility available for redeployment elsewhere.

Documentation

Annexure 2 defines the deliverable register: PFDs and P&IDs per well, area and central facility, HAZID/HAZOP reports, piping material and valve specs, plot plans, isometrics, pipeline design and crossing drawings, electrical and ICSS deliverables, and the 3D model review.

Reporting

Daily operations report at 06:00 and an update at 18:00, daily signed time sheets, HSE reports with immediate notification of incidents, plus RCA reports on request. All operating parameters archived at 30-second intervals.

Key personnel

Dedicated representatives permanently in Missan, contactable 24 hours, with at least 15 years of industry experience. Company may require replacement of any incompetent or negligent person within five days at the Contractor’s cost.

Contractor assumptions on this site

  • Skid and package counts are a bottom-up estimate from the concept flowsheets, not a vendor equipment list. Expect ±10%.
  • Utilities, power and the export tank farm are shared between the three trains; process, flare and metering are dedicated.
  • Three flares — one per train — are shown; a single common flare with segregated headers is an acceptable alternative if the radiation study allows.
  • Sand handling is at the EPF inlet (Option B). Wellsite sand traps would remove three packages.
  • The 3D model is a massing study: no structural, blast or hazardous-area verification is implied.

Open items for the Company

  • Confirmed well list, sustainable rates and connection dates per reservoir.
  • Approved simultaneous feed cases — GOR, water cut, H₂S/CO₂, salinity, sand, wax and asphaltene.
  • Export Option A or B, and the Halfaya receiving pressure envelope.
  • Water disposal destination, permitted quality, capacity and readiness date; evaporation pond design.
  • Permitted flaring conditions and emission limits; gas utilisation or sulphur recovery requirement.
  • Stripping-gas backup philosophy and the production-reduction rule if it is unavailable.
  • Whether the 5,000 bopd Yamama unit uses electrostatic desalting (Scope of Work) or filter-coalescers (Execution Strategy).
  • Power generation fuel: diesel, associated gas, or a defined mix — and who owns fuel supply and custody metering.
  • Battery limit and responsibility matrix, including temporary CPF-site storage and its utilities.
